# Living With an E-Ink Smartphone for Six Months
Like most people, I use my phone a lot more than I intend to. That is generally by design. After all, social media and video apps are built to capture and keep your attention.

There are solutions to this of course. Deleting the offending apps, setting timers or app limits and of course just good ol' self control. However, it's a constant battle. Knowing how easy it is to work around the self-imposed limitations has always been an issue for me.

With all this in mind, I decided that I wanted to move away from a traditional smart phone. The very first phone I owned was a flip phone and I look back at its lack of functionality with fondness. However, in the modern age that idea was just too impractical. How do I access two-factor authentication codes, check my bank, navigate with maps, or message friends on Signal and WhatsApp?

Minimalist phones do exist, you may even have been advertised one if you're in certain online circles. However, I find that they often impose too many restrictions. Generally they only provide access to the same features of an older brick phone. You can call, you can text and you can set an alarm. Some allow for a few more functionalities like maps but it tends to be a very locked down device.  I personally find it too impractical to use even if I'm happy the niche exists.

What I wanted was a middle ground, a hardware solution that I couldn't work around but could still access the apps I needed on an everyday basis. That's where the Bigme Hibreak Pro comes in. It's a smartphone running a custom version of Android 14 with an E-Ink screen, the same screen that a lot of the Kindles use. All of its limitations revolve around the device's screen - everything else functions as a normal smartphone.

I used it as my main phone for six months before it suffered an unfortunate accident and died. I ultimately decided to not return to using one due to a few issues I'll talk about but I hope that the technology matures over the next 5 years.  With more stability I'd love to return to using one as my daily driver.

## The Good, The Bad and the Ugly

The way that the phone disincentivises you into using it to waste time is both its strength and its weakness - the screen. It can get a surprisingly decent refresh rate, good enough to make watching the occasional video worth it, but scrolling through Reddit or Instagram? Forget it. It isn't unusable but I personally always found it unpleasant to do.

Of course it is black and white (although colour E-Ink screens do exist) so there are issues caused by that too. A friend sends you a nice photo? You may need to see it on a computer if you actually want to know what they sent. What's more unexpected how much you're effected by the design of some websites and apps - so many are simply non-functional on a black and white screen. There isn't a good way to fix this either from the phone side. If I wanted to check certain apps out that used dark colours and contrast, then I was simply out of luck.

Even apps with great colourblind settings can run into problems on an E-Ink screen. There is only so much you can do to differentiate shades of grey. It has also had an effect on the way I think about design. I've become much more aware that colour alone shouldn't be carrying important information. If removing colour makes something confusing, then it probably needed another way of communicating that information in the first place.

It also just isn't that fast. It's far from a flagship device and it runs older hardware so you often get a barely passable experience using it. One major benefit though is that you are no longer beholden to the sun. Do you want to check your messages at midday in the blazing sun? No problem at all, zero need to hide in the shade to do so.

The company that makes them, Bigme, mainly make e-readers. Unfortunately what that means is that the software, such as the custom launcher that the phone uses by default, is pretty sub optimal. Bugs are plentiful, functions will fail to work after an update and the app launcher likes to reorganize itself based on whatever mood it woke up on that day.

## The Future

It's too new and niche for me to really recommend the phone to anyone at this stage. With a few years of R&D and some much needed modernization, however, I personally think that it could be the best phone type on the market for those looking to step away from a normal smartphone.

While I had it for those six months, I found that I would spend less on my phone. Without colour and all of the phone's issues, the urge to check it just slowly went away.  A lot of websites are simply less interesting when seen through a black and white screen.

This was the ultimate goal so in that, the phone did its job perfectly.
